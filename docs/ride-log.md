# Ride log (working notes, in the creator's words, lightly tidied)

The story of the 47–48-day ride, as told by Gaurav. These notes feed the Story page, the circuit
pages and the Gems. Dates in brackets come from the photo album; the rest is as told.

## Before the ride (June–July 2026)
- Packed up in Bangalore, went home, then to Delhi for a couple of days.
- Took the bike for a service round and made it ready; a couple of days later washed it and rode
  straight north. The ride started at India Gate, Delhi.
- Mid-points on the way north: photos exist.
- A week in Manali before the ride proper.
- Hamta Pass: a 6-day trek with the crew; heavy rain, reached the summit; "a great experience to be
  with such great people" (many photos). Then Chandratal. After that, alone [album starts 17 Jul].

## Circuit 1 — Zanskar (as ridden: Manali → Darcha → Padum → Kargil → Lamayuru → Leh, then Leh → Zangla → Leh; 29 Jul – 4 Aug)

The circuit is defined in this direction on the site: it starts in Manali and ends in Leh, with the
Leh–Zangla–Leh ride on the new highway as its last leg.
- Manali → Shinku La [29 Jul] → Gonbo Rangjon [29 Jul]: stayed; "magnificent mountain, great local
  people, just a couple of houses".
- → Phuktal Monastery [30 Jul] (before Padum) → Padum [30 Jul]: a day or two in a specific hotel
  (name to come).
- → a couple of other monasteries → Nun Kun, Drang-Drung glacier (Pensi La, Rangdum, Satso Lake
  [31 Jul]) → Kargil [31 Jul]: 3 days.
- From Kargil: Hunderman, the last village of India, with Gilgit-Baltistan in view; all on the
  bike, off-road [1 Aug]. The valleys around Kargil: Aryan valley, Suru valley.
- → Lamayuru [2 Aug] (Mulbekh, Fotu La on the way): dusty, windy, the natural formations beautiful;
  stayed a day. → many passes and shades of mountain (Likir, Basgo [3 Aug]) → Leh [4 Aug].
  First circuit ends. 5 days in Leh.
- From Leh: a one-way ride on the new Padum–Leh highway (built in the last ~2 years), Leh → Zangla
  Palace and back, not on to Padum (already seen).

### Leh, and the villages before it
- Five days in Leh, several stays tried; Rainbow Hostel was the one that stood out. Leh Palace was great.
  Food places and places to explore nearby: Gaurav will list them at the end.
- Between Lamayuru and Leh, small villages worth the detour: Likir, Mulbekh, Basgo, Alchi: "sweet
  hidden gems". Lamayuru's monastery is great; an art event was on at the time, with artists'
  installations around the village (name sounded like "Sa bin Ali": to confirm).

## Circuit 2 — Nubra (as ridden: Leh → Khardung La → Turtuk → Diskit → Siachen BC → Wari La → Leh; ~6–14 Aug)
- Leh → Khardung La [6 Aug]: the pass full of snow, first snowfall of the ride. Passed through Diskit
  without stopping, straight on to Turtuk.
- Turtuk [photos 9 Aug]: 4 days. A village on the India–Pakistan border; about 20 km on is Thang, the
  international border village. From Turtuk you see the peaks in Pakistan; the Pakistani army is in the
  mountains ("you can't see them, they can see you"). No road into the village: park, cross a footbridge,
  everything on foot; four days without the bike. The Balti Kitchen, 200–300 m in from the bridge, a
  ten-year-old kitchen, really good. Balti Museum; old protected Balti houses, under the earth, showing how
  people lived through the winters. Stay name to come. (He said "4 weeks" once; it was 4 days.)
- Back to Diskit [12 Aug]: 2 days. Off season, so a hotel with dinner and breakfast for ₹1,500, a full
  course meal cooked to order, veg or non-veg, a beautiful garden: name to come. The monastery is really
  beautiful; the big statue (he was told it is an old king's, not the Buddha's: confirm).
- Day ride from Diskit [13 Aug]: late start about 11, the owner insisted; Siachen Base Camp by about 3,
  a good 120 km one way, 3–4 hours, barely anyone, shops and stays closed. On the way back, the hot spring
  at Panamik (~50 km from Diskit): crazy hot water in the middle of an icy desert, a half-hour shower.
  Back to Diskit about 8 PM in rain, 1–2 hours through the desert in the dark: the only night riding of
  the trip. Exhausted.
- Diskit → Wari La → Hemis → Leh [14 Aug]: the bridge on the Shyok road to Pangong was closed, so Wari La
  instead of Khardung La again (half an hour more). Felt like a homecoming. End of circuit 2.

### Circuit 2, answers and more
- Turtuk stay: Maha Guest House. Don't stay near the road: walk up the stream into the village; the upper
  village above the stream is the beautiful part. The rooftop at Duktuk Hotel is worth it, best at sunset.
  The footbridge is the Razabe Turtuk Bridge (spellings as heard).
- Diskit stay: Hotel Karakoram. The Siachen run went "via Warshi and all".
- Panamik: the hot spring is built and kept by the locals, ₹50. There are hidden hot streams off the
  route, but alone and off season he stayed with the built one.
- Lamayuru's art event: the Sa Biennale.

## Circuit 3 — Changthang (as ridden: Leh → Chang La → Pangong → Hanle → Nurbula → Umling La → Demchok → Nyoma → Karzok → Manali; 16–21 Aug)
"The biggest and most important circuit, the one I came for: the summit at Umling La." 2–3 more days in Leh first.
- Leh → (Hemis skipped, already seen) → Chemrey monastery → Chang La top [16 Aug]: he had stood there two years
  earlier in summer; a memory of growth, of how life changed and around whom. → Pangong; stayed in Maan
  village ("one of the most beautiful villages"), a bit of snow outside, great views and a great ride around
  the lake.
- → Hanle [17 Aug] (Chushul, Tsaga La on the way): met riders from Maharashtra on the road. Pangong to Hanle,
  one of the most beautiful roads to ride. Overnight; the astronomical site.
- Summit day [18 Aug]: Hanle → the Nurbula top route: no road, a 75 km off-road climb from Hanle to 5,200 m
  at Nurbula top → another 50–60 km → Umling La. "Every metre up you realise you're about as high as anyone
  on earth right now bar a few thousand on summits, and it's not summit season." (He says 6,000 m; the
  published height is about 5,800 m: the site shows the terrain model's figure, not 6,000.) The highest
  drivable road in the world. Mig La was about to open but demolished and army-restricted; maybe 2028.
- Instead of back to Hanle: down the new road from Umling La to Demchok (good road, not off-road). The
  Demchok camp, the India–China border: the Chinese village about 100 m away, officials moving between
  bunkers. An hour's rest, then Demchok → Nyoma on the new highway: not on Google Maps; Mappls had it
  (Mappls is good in the north). Airtel worked everywhere; buy a local SIM in Leh, or Padum if coming from
  Himachal. The Indus valley road: Chinese mountains on the right, the Indus, the road, Indian mountains on
  the left, ~100 km dead straight, empty valley, rivers kilometres wide, the true scale of the mountains.
  "The highlight of my entire bike ride; the most beautiful place I rode." (He said "between India and
  Nepal"; China.)
- Nyoma about 3 pm, fuel nearly gone, filled up; the people at the pump said the road on was well built, so
  on to Karzok (70–80 km) via the Mahe Bridge cut. On the way Kyagar Tso: with the snow just right, three
  peaks behind the valley look like three pyramids; not a single person by the lake.
- Karzok, Tso Moriri [18–19 Aug]: 2 days. The most beautiful stay of the whole ride (Turtuk second, Kalpa
  third, to come). Hotels super expensive; only 10–15 homestays. A small cabin with an uncle, an aunt and
  their grandson; ate and slept with them for two days, by the lake, the landscape, the snow; Mentok
  (Kangri) above.
- Karzok → the Leh–Manali highway [20 Aug]: went up to Taglang La and came back the same way, then south:
  Gata Loops ("phenomenal"), Suraj Tal ("incredible"), Jispa, Sissu, the Ladakh–Himachal border, the Atal
  Tunnel → Manali [21 Aug]. End of circuit 3.

## Circuit 4 — Spiti (as ridden: Manali → Jibhi → Jalori Pass → Kalpa → Nako → Gue → Tabo → Kaza → Chitkul → Narkanda → Shimla → Chandigarh → Delhi; 21/22–31 Aug)
- Manali → back onto the Delhi–Manali highway, then off it onto the inner road to Jibhi [22 Aug]. Wet in the
  last ten minutes of the ride in, so an extra day: 2 days in Jibhi, everything washed and dried, good food,
  local drinks (passion fruit and other fruit juices).
- Jibhi → Jalori Pass → down to the lower ground → a bridge where the highway from Shimla meets the road
  (name to confirm) → Kalpa [23/24 Aug]: 4 days. Third-best stay of the ride: a small B&B, owner away, a key
  locker for the entry; a village you can walk in half an hour, four days of it under Kinnaur Kailash. One
  night at Zostel Kalpa: one of the best views in Kalpa, "stay there a week".
- Kalpa → Nako Lake → "Ka village" (which: confirm) → Gue Monastery [27 Aug]: about 10 km off the main road,
  20–25 minutes on a rough on-and-off road; the old mummy; phones pick up China's time there. → Tabo
  Monastery: unlike any other, a different architecture and different deities, the buildings all covered in
  mud, brownish. → Kaza [27–28 Aug]: 2 days. A day trip: Hikkim, the highest post office in the world (posted
  a letter to his parents; it never arrived; 20–30% don't, and he was ready for that), Langza (the iconic
  Buddha), Komic (India's highest village), Key Monastery; a fun ride in bad weather, dusty, windy, sunny
  and cold at once.
- The Chandratal/Kunzum road from Kaza to the Atal Tunnel (80–90 km) skipped: the only bad patch left in
  Spiti, under construction, maybe ready by 2028; and Chandratal had been seen after Hamta. So back down the
  same road, skipping the villages already stayed in → Chitkul [29 Aug]: the last village of India on the
  Himachal side; a day or so; really peaceful, really beautiful.
- Chitkul → Narkanda [30 Aug]: not well after getting wet over the previous days; a day there; "not top
  three, but for sure top five"; regretted not staying one more day.
- Narkanda → Shimla: Mashobra, a village close to his heart; Shimla → the four-lane highway to Chandigarh,
  "so sexy to cruise on a bike" → Chandigarh [31 Aug]: met his sisters → left about 4 pm → Delhi by about
  10 at night. Project Altitude complete: about 5,200 km, the four valleys of Zanskar, Nubra, Changthang
  and Spiti.
